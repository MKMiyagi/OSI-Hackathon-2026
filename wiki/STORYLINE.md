# Storyline (one page)

Results use `data/splits/val.csv`, a **reused development set**, or, where stated, pooled train+val outer folds (nested CV); the target is the DFT-derived Chen proxy `hv_chen`, not measured hardness. The previously reported secondary metrics were pre-registered in `wiki/DECISIONS.md` before they were computed; the chemical-family analyses below are exploratory diagnostics; figures in `wiki/figures/metrics_*.png`, tables in `ledger/metrics_extra*.csv`.

**Problem.** Can a compact, composition-only formula emulate `hv_chen` (Chen 2011 applied to DFT B and G) about as well as the Tehrani-style pipeline (Magpie SVR -> log B, log G -> Chen)? The labels are Chen's output, so this is emulation of a proxy, not a claim to beat Chen or to predict measured hardness.

**Frozen protocol.** Val MAE in GPa via `eval/score.py`; 70/15/15 split grouped by element set (train and val share none); a 9-seed no-descriptor reference arm; a descriptor "improves" only if the c<=12 mean drops >= 0.08 GPa and the descriptor is selected in most seeds. The test set has not been read. Comparators: train-median constant 5.044 GPa, SVR 2.839 GPa.

**Formulas sit between the constant and the SVR.** At c<=12 the mean selected val MAE is 3.980 GPa for Magpie-only and 3.684 GPa with d_023, versus 2.839 for the SVR and 5.044 for the constant: closing 48% and 62% of the gap. Fraction of the constant-to-SVR gap closed, (const - formula)/(const - SVR), mean over seeds of the best equation within the budget:

| arm | c<=9 | c<=12 | c<=15 | MASE vs SVR at c<=12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Magpie only (9 seeds) | 39% | 48% | 51% | 1.40 |
| + d_023 (3 seeds) | 61% | 62% | 63% | 1.30 |

On reused val, the c8 d_023 formula closes 61% of the gap, with a descriptive cluster-bootstrap interval of [43.9%, 76.5%]; its MAE is 1.30 times the SVR's [1.18, 1.45]. The intervals do not account for adaptive selection or optimizer variation. All 1,032 recorded equations have higher val MAE than the SVR; the best ratio is 1.27. The log(Hv+3) metric gives the same picture (0.44 vs SVR 0.33 vs constant 0.57); 15 of the 986 non-smoke Pareto equations (excluding the 42 using MeltingT/SpaceGroup) predict <= -3 for one val row (none is a d_023 form).

**Where they fail.** (i) Binned MAE: in the 5-15 GPa bin (n = 150), c8 approximately ties the constant (3.28 vs 3.31); c5 and c9 are worse (4.00 and 3.59). All three trail the SVR (2.62) in every bin below 25 GPa. (ii) Ranking: Spearman 0.56-0.61 vs 0.79 for the SVR; precision at the top decile 0.53-0.58 vs 0.61. These are 20-22 vs 23 hits among 38; the uncertainty of the differences has not been quantified. (iii) Tail: for the 17 val rows >= 20 GPa, c8 and the SVR have mean signed errors of -6.33 and -7.30 GPa (both underpredict) and MAEs of 7.26 and 7.52 GPa. At >= 25 GPa the observed MAEs are 8.11 and 10.62, from only ten rows, and the split has one row >= 40 GPa, so this supports no superhard-screening claim in either direction. Stratifying by the true value flatters flat predictors in the middle bins; it is a diagnostic, not a ranking.

**The d_023 lead.** Cohesive-energy density (d_023 = 160.2 * mean E_coh / volume per atom, GPa) lowers the c<=12 mean from 3.980 to 3.684 (-0.30, about 5 reference sd), is selected in 3/3 seeds, and survives a dimensional-consistency constraint (c9 `(d_023/1.367)/(chi + NdValence/NsValence)`, 3.697). Caveats: the three seeds converge to the same equations (search stability, not three confirmations); d_023 was screened on train and scored once on val before its cycle, so it is not val-blind; the c8 form adds a count to a GPa quantity; composition means cannot separate polymorphs. Both cohesive energy and volume are composition averages of elemental properties, not compound cohesive energy or compound volume, and dimensional consistency does not establish a physical mechanism. "About five reference sd" measures optimizer variation, not five-sigma predictive evidence.

**Robustness (nested grouped CV).** To test whether the d_023 gain depends on the single val split, train+val were pooled (2,120 rows, 1,406 element-set groups; the quarantined test file was not read), repartitioned into five element-set-grouped outer folds, and the equation was chosen in each fold by inner-validation MAE only and scored once on the held-out fold (cycle 033, `wiki/NESTED_CV_D023.md`). d_023 was selected in 5/5 folds at every window and beat Magpie-only in 5/5 folds at every window (paired mean gain 0.42 / 0.18 / 0.27 GPa at c<=9 / 12 / 15, against 0.50 / 0.30 / 0.28 on val), and the c8 form was re-derived in 4 of 5 folds at c<=9. Fold-mean outer-fold MAE at c<=12 is 3.61 GPa (d_023) vs 3.79 (Magpie only), 2.81 (SVR) and 4.89 (constant): 62% of the gap closed vs 53%, MASE vs SVR 1.29 vs 1.36. Caveats: d_023 as a feature choice was screened on train labels and scored once on val, and val is inside the pooled set, so this is recurrence under repartition of reused development data, not independent validation; the c<=12 gain is smaller than on val; each cell is one PySR fit, folds share about three quarters of their training rows, and the spread across folds is fold heterogeneity, not optimizer or selection uncertainty; the SVR uses 132 features and separate log B / log G supervision, and an SVR fitted on only the inner-training rows used for PySR fitting scores 2.95 (equation selection additionally uses inner-validation labels). Adding d_031 (metal fraction) on top of d_023 changes nothing (-0.007 at c<=12, +0.025 at c<=15). The matching 62% on val and in the nested folds is descriptive, not a replication with an interval.

**Where it fails and why.** By chemical family on val (fixed grouping rules, diagnostics only; `wiki/RESIDUALS_D023.md`), oxides are the one family where the c8 d_023 formula is worse than the constant (MAE 4.19 vs 3.94; SVR 2.23) and it overpredicts by 2.6 GPa on average (n = 36). Our reading, untested, is that oxides combine high cohesive-energy density (strongly bound O, small volume) with low Chen hardness because G/B is low, a shear or ductility signature that an energy-per-volume proxy cannot see. Within the binary carbides/nitrides/borides (n = 14) the small mean bias (+0.6) is cancellation: ZrB2 and TaN are underpredicted by 19 and 15 GPa while BaC2 and CoN are overpredicted by 15 and 10; ZrB2 and BaC2 both have B/C/N fraction 2/3, and TaN and CoN both 1/2, so those fractions alone cannot separate them; adding d_031 showed no consistent incremental benefit in the runs performed (a negative result for that descriptor, not proof that no feature could). A hypothesized remedy, a shear or Pugh-ratio proxy, could not be tested as specified: elemental shear moduli are missing for B, C, N, O, F, Si, P, S, As, Ge, Ga, In, Cl, Br, I in pymatgen and in the fallback handbook tables we searched (a limited search, not proof of absence everywhere), so dropping those rows removes 59-68% of train and val, including 63 of 86 train rows >= 20 GPa, and `eval/score.py` cannot score NaN features. On the 119 val rows where the values do exist (almost all intermetallics and heavier compounds), neither d_030 nor d_028 adds a consistent gain over d_023 (+0.013 and -0.045 at c<=12, below the 0.08 threshold), so the question stays open exactly where the errors are. Caveats: per-family n is small (oxides 36, carbides/nitrides/borides 14) and from one reused val set; the families are rule-based groupings; and part of the failure may not be fixable at all, since some rows with the same composition features have very different targets (the SVR, which is trained to predict B and G from composition, also misses CoN, V5Si3 and Al3Ir).

**What Thursday's measured check will and won't show.** The Zhang et al. pool (529 compositions, 364 with element sets absent from train+val) answers a different question: how our direct hardness formulas compare with *measured* Vickers hardness. The load window is already frozen in `eval/external_zhang.py` (headline: median over 0.49-1.96 N; secondary: median at >= 4.9 N); evaluable counts per window and predictor must be reported. It is a one-shot check, not for selection, and it will not change any number above. It will not establish that any formula is a law, and because the formulas were fit to Chen's output they inherit Chen's own error against experiment. The one-shot test-set run measures proxy emulation on unseen compounds, not measured hardness.
